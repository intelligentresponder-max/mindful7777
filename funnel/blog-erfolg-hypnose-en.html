what the research says

Hypnosis and success — that sounds like a mix of self-help shelf and fairground tent. Both would be wrong. What actually happens when people work systematically with hypnosis techniques is more sober, better founded and ultimately more convincing than any promise.

Here you get an honest overview — what studies say, which athletes use hypnosis, and how you can integrate it into your everyday life. Without promises of salvation.

What research actually shows

State of research · 2025

The effectiveness of clinical hypnosis is documented in over 200 empirical studies — for areas such as anxiety, pain, sleep and stress reduction. A meta-analysis by Erich Flammer covers 188 controlled studies with over 10,000 participants. The effect sizes are remarkable in certain areas — especially relaxation, sleep and pain management.

Sources: IFHE Berlin · Flammer meta-analysis (1887–2009) · Milton Erickson Gesellschaft (MEG) · Frontiers in Psychology, Clinical Hypnosis Series 2023–2025

That does not mean hypnosis is equally well supported for every application. In sport psychology the evidence is promising, but not yet as methodologically robust as for medical indications. The honest assessment: a lot of good experience, solid theory, a growing body of studies.

Why 90% is mental — and what that means

In sport psychology one figure is often quoted: 90% of athletic performance is mental — once the physical baseline is reached. This assessment goes back to Soviet sport scientists, including Dr. Vladimir Platonov, who was the first to systematically integrate hypnosis into Olympic training programs. Today sport psychologists worldwide treat mental techniques as a matter of course in training.

Sources: Platonov, V.N. · DiscoveringHypnosis.com · Frontiers in Psychology (2021) — “The Application of Hypnosis in Sports”

What hypnosis can specifically support in success

Focus & concentration

Under hypnosis the prefrontal cortex becomes quieter — the area responsible for critical thinking and self-control. This allows a deeper state of focus related to the experience of “flow”. Many athletes report that mental relaxation techniques before competitions can reduce nervousness and sharpen concentration.

Mental rehearsal (anticipating success)

In the hypnotic state the brain is especially receptive to visualization — inner images that feel as real as actual experiences. When you imagine a successful penalty kick, a clean lift or a calm performance under hypnosis, you activate the same neural patterns as when actually doing it. That is not esoterics — that is neuroscience.

“Hypnosis is not a magic word. It’s a tool — and it works when you use it regularly and correctly.”

Recovery & regeneration

Deep relaxation activates the parasympathetic nervous system — the counterpart of the stress-hormone system. In this state the body regenerates faster. Many athletes report that regular relaxation audio improves their sleep quality and that they wake up more rested on training days.

Changing habits

In behavioral psychology, hypnosis is increasingly being studied as support for changing habits. A study published in 2024 by the Milton Erickson Gesellschaft shows that hypnotherapy for smoking cessation can be about as effective as cognitive behavioral therapy — a promising result that motivates further research.

What hypnosis cannot do

Hypnosis cannot replace hard work. It cannot compensate for poor training. It is no substitute for coaching, nutrition or sleep. And it is definitely no substitute for psychotherapy or medical treatment for diagnosed conditions.

What it can do: support a foundation you have already built — calmer, deeper, more consistent.
