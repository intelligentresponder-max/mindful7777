Learn self-hypnosis —
the first step

Self-hypnosis sounds more complicated than it is. Most people think you need talent, training or weeks of practice. In reality self-hypnosis is a learnable technique — no different from deep breathing or stretching. All you need is a quiet place, 10 minutes and the willingness to engage.

“Everyone already knows self-hypnosis — from reading a book, daydreaming, falling asleep. The difference: you learn to induce this state deliberately.”

What self-hypnosis actually is

Self-hypnosis is the independent induction of a relaxed, focused state of awareness without outside guidance. You guide yourself — through breathing exercises, body awareness, inner images or repetition.

In this state your subconscious is more accessible — the part of you that steers habits, automates reactions and holds emotional patterns. That is what makes self-hypnosis interesting: not as magic, but as a more direct way of communicating with yourself.

Which techniques are there?

🫁
Breath focus

The simplest entry technique. You observe your breath — without changing it. After 2–3 minutes it slows down by itself. Attention moves inward.

🌀
Eye fixation

A spot on the wall or a candle flame. You fix your gaze until your eyes become heavy. The drowsiness is the doorway into the induction state.

🏔️
Staircase image

You imagine walking down a staircase — deeper with every step. A classic deepening technique from hypnotherapy, easy to apply yourself.

💪
Progressive relaxation

You move inwardly through your body — from the feet to the head — inviting each area to relax. Very body-oriented, works quickly and reliably.

Your first self-hypnosis — step by step

01

Preparation — 2 min

A quiet place. No noise, no phone. Sit or lie comfortably. You don’t have to do anything “right” — just arrive. Imagine you have 10 minutes that belong only to you.

02

Set the breath as an anchor — 3 min

Breathe in for 4 seconds, hold for 7, breathe out for 8. This 4-7-8 technique directly activates the parasympathetic nervous system. Three to four rounds are enough. Then simply let the breath flow and observe it.

03

Deepening through a body scan — 3 min

Start at the feet. Say inwardly: “My feet are heavy and warm. I relax.” Then calves, knees, thighs, belly, chest, shoulders, arms, hands, neck, face. No pressure — just an invitation.

04

Plant the suggestion — 2 min

Now say to yourself inwardly — calm, warm, determined — a sentence you wish for. Phrased positively, in the present tense. For example: “I sleep deeply and rested.” or “I move calmly through the day.” Repeat it 3–5 times.

05

Return — 1 min

Count inwardly from 5 to 1. With every number you become more awake. At 1: open your eyes, stretch your arms, breathe deeply. Done. You are fully back.

Frequently asked

“What if I don’t see images or feel anything?”

That is completely normal — especially at the beginning. Self-hypnosis is not a visual experience you have to force. Sometimes it is just a quiet settling. That counts. Access gets easier with practice — like any other training.

When and how often to practice?

Daily short sessions of 10 minutes are more effective than rare long ones. Morning after waking or evening before sleep are natural anchor points — in these transition phases the brain is more open to relaxation states anyway.

Many people report a noticeable change after 7–14 days of regular practice — more inner calm, easier falling asleep, a calmer way of handling stressful situations. Not a promise — an experience you can make for yourself.
