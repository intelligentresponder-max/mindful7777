The 7-Protocol Stack

Seven short protocols you can use one by one or as a stack. Each has a trigger, a duration and a metric, so a feeling becomes a reproducible result.

How to use it

  1. Pick one protocol and run it daily for 7 days. Only then add the next.
  2. Measure before and after: pulse, HRV (if you wear a tracker) or your subjective tension from 1 to 10.
  3. Attach the protocol to a fixed cue (anchor). The more consistently the same cue is paired with the same routine, the faster the response becomes available on demand. That is classical conditioning, as described by Pavlov.
01

Presence Reset

Bring your attention back

Trigger
When switching between two tasks (meeting to meeting, phone to work).
Duration
60 seconds
Protocol
Name three things you see, three you hear, three points of body contact (feet, seat, hands). Then one task, one screen.
Measure
Focus self-rating 1–10 before and after the switch.
02

Morning Stack

The start shapes the day

Trigger
Right after getting up, before looking at your phone.
Duration
10–15 minutes
Protocol
One glass of water, 2 minutes of mobility, 3 minutes of the breath protocol (no. 03), then write the day's most important task in one sentence.
Measure
Number of days in a row. Goal: 7.
03

Breath Protocol

The fastest route to the vagus nerve

Trigger
Before conversations, after stress, in the evening before sleep.
Duration
3 minutes
Protocol
Inhale for 4 seconds, exhale for 6. The longer exhale activates the parasympathetic system via the vagus nerve. 18 breaths are enough.
Measure
Pulse before and after; with a tracker, add morning HRV.
04

Input Windows

What you take in drives your nervous system

Trigger
Two fixed slots per day for news and social media; no caffeine after 2 pm.
Duration
Ongoing
Protocol
Keep the phone in airplane mode or in another room outside the windows. 60 screen-free minutes before bed.
Measure
Screen time per day; minutes to fall asleep.
05

Movement Reset

Body before mind

Trigger
After 90 minutes of sitting or when thoughts loop.
Duration
5 minutes
Protocol
Stand up, 20 squats or brisk walking, then 5 deep breaths as in no. 03. A walk outdoors counts double: daylight stabilizes your sleep-wake rhythm.
Measure
Steps or minutes of movement; subjective tension 1–10.
06

Sleep Anchor

Falling asleep as a trainable response

Trigger
Every evening in bed, always with the same cue.
Duration
90 seconds
Protocol
A fixed cue (for example the same sock on one foot) is paired every evening with breath protocol no. 03. After a few weeks the cue alone works as the signal. This is the principle of the Sock-Anchor Protocol.
Measure
Log time to fall asleep for 3 weeks and compare the averages.
07

Calibration

Weekly review

Trigger
Sunday evening.
Duration
10 minutes
Protocol
Answer three questions in writing: Which protocol ran? What changed measurably? What do I drop or change next week? Then add or adjust exactly one protocol.
Measure
One line per week in a fixed document. After 4 weeks you see the trend.

Once your stack is in place

The next step is a protocol you actually follow through on. The smaller the start, the more likely you are to stay with it. The Sock-Anchor is the shortest: 90 seconds per evening.
